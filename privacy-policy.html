Find My Stuff

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 21 October 2026 · App ID: app.dotinfiny.findmystuff

Find My Stuff (“the app”) is provided by Muhammad Asher Ansari (“the developer”, “we”). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app on Android.

In short

  • Your inventory — item names, notes, photos, places and containers — stays on your device. We never receive it.
  • There is no account, no sign-in and no inventory server. All inventory features work offline.
  • The app uses Google services that do use the internet: AdMob for ads, and Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics for crash reports and usage statistics. These receive device and usage information, never your inventory content.

1. What the app does

The app stores what you record — item names, notes, photos, places and containers — so you can look up where you said something is. It shows the location you recorded. It does not detect objects or track where they actually are.

2. Your inventory stays on your device

Your inventory is stored in the app’s private storage on your device. There is no account, no sign-in and no inventory server, so we do not receive, see or store your items, notes, photos, places or containers. All inventory features, including search and QR scanning, work without an internet connection.

3. Camera and photos

The camera is used only when you choose to take a photo or scan a QR label. QR codes are decoded on your device. Photos you take or choose are copied, resized and saved in the app’s private storage; the app does not upload them. Camera permission is requested only when you first use one of these features, and you can still choose photos from your gallery without it.

4. QR labels

A printed label contains only a random container ID. It contains no names, notes or photos. Scanning a label shows contents only on a device that holds the matching inventory. If a scanned code contains a web link, the app shows it as text and does not open it.

5. Backups you create

When you choose Back up, the app writes a ZIP file with your records and photos to a location you pick, for example your device storage or a cloud folder you select. Backup files are not encrypted: anyone who can access the file can read it, and the terms and privacy policy of any storage service you choose apply to it. Restoring a backup replaces the inventory in the app.

6. Android backup and device transfer

The app opts out of Android cloud backup, so Android does not upload your inventory to your Google account. When you move to a new phone with Android’s device-to-device transfer (Android 12 and later), your inventory, photos and app settings may be copied directly to the new device.

7. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app is free and shows ads from Google AdMob, which uses the internet. Google and its partners may collect and process information such as your device’s advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from it, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. See Google’s Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Where required by law (for example in the EEA, the UK and some US states), you are asked for consent through Google’s consent tool, and you can change your choices later in the app under Settings → Ad privacy choices when that option is shown. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. Your inventory content is never given to the advertising SDK. If ads can’t load, the app keeps working normally.

8. Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, sends a crash report so we can find and fix the problem. A report contains technical details such as the error and where in the app it happened, the device model, Android version, app version and a random installation identifier. It never includes your items, notes, photos, place or container names, or backups.

9. Usage statistics (Firebase Analytics)

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand, in aggregate, how the app is used and to improve it. It automatically collects information such as app opens and sessions, screens viewed, app version, device model, Android version, language, an app-instance identifier, approximate location (country or region) derived from your IP address and, where available, your advertising ID. The app does not send your inventory content — item names, notes, photos, places or containers — to Analytics.

More information: Privacy and Security in Firebase. Google keeps Crashlytics and Analytics data according to its retention settings for Firebase.

10. Permissions

  • Camera — only to take item photos and scan QR labels, requested when first used.
  • Internet / network state — used by ads, crash reports and usage statistics. Inventory features do not need it.

The app does not request access to your contacts, precise location, microphone, call logs or SMS.

11. Contacting support

If you use Contact support, your email app opens a message addressed to us, pre-filled only with the app version and Android version. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself. We use your email address and message only to reply to you. Inventory records, photos, backups and device identifiers are never attached automatically.

12. Deleting your data

  • You can delete items, containers and places in the app at any time.
  • Uninstalling the app removes the inventory stored in it.
  • Backup files you created are not removed automatically; delete them where you saved them.
  • For data held by Google for ads, crash reports or usage statistics, use your Android ad settings and Google’s privacy tools, or contact us and we will help with requests we can act on.

13. Your choices and rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information, or to object to or restrict its use. Because we do not receive your inventory, most of your information is under your control on your device. For questions or requests about information processed through the Google services described above, contact us at the address below.

14. Children

The app is a general-audience household tool and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

15. Changes and contact

We may update this policy when the app changes. The effective date at the top shows the latest version. Significant changes will also be reflected in the app’s in-app policy.
